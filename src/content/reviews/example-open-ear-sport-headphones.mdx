Running in the dark means listening as much as looking. Headphones that seal your ears shut trade awareness for sound, and on a busy road that is a poor bargain. The Example Open-Ear Sport Headphones take the opposite approach, resting just outside the ear canal so the world stays audible.

## Fit & Feel

Each bud weighs about nine grams, and the flexible hook distributes that weight evenly over the top of the ear. Nothing goes inside the canal, so there is no pressure build-up or itchiness on long runs. We wore them alongside sunglasses, a cap and a winter headband without conflict.

Stability is excellent. Strides, downhill running and a few frantic sprints for a changing pedestrian light did not shift them at all.

The matte finish shrugged off sweat and a couple of properly wet runs, and the IP55 rating gave us no reason to worry. The charging case is slim enough for a jacket pocket, though it is a touch too large for most running shorts. Magnetic charging contacts snap the buds into place reliably, even in the half-light of a hallway before sunrise.

## On the Run

Physical buttons on each bud handle play, pause, skip and volume, and they are easy to find with cold fingers. Pairing is quick, and multipoint let us switch between phone and watch without digging through menus. Connection stayed solid even with the phone in a back waistband pocket.

Ten hours of playback means most runners will only charge once a week, and a ten-minute top-up in the case adds roughly two hours.

## Sound & Awareness

This is where the design pays off. With music at a moderate volume we could clearly hear approaching cars, bike bells and conversation. That awareness comes at a cost: bass is light, and in heavy wind or next to loud traffic, quieter tracks get lost. Podcasts and audiobooks fare better than bass-heavy playlists. Mids and vocals, on the other hand, are clean and well separated, and the companion app's EQ preset for outdoor running adds a welcome lift to the low end without muddying the detail.

Sound leakage is noticeable above about 70 percent volume, so these are less ideal for a quiet gym or office. Call quality is clear in calm conditions but suffers in strong wind.

## Who It's For

If most of your miles are on roads, shared paths or anywhere you need to hear what is coming, the Example Open-Ear Sport Headphones are an easy recommendation. They are also a good fit for runners who find in-ear buds uncomfortable or unstable. If you mostly run on a treadmill or a quiet track and want rich, punchy sound, a sealed sport earbud will serve you better.
