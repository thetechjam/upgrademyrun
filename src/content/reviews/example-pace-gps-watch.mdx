A good running watch should do two things: tell you the truth about your run, and get out of the way while you do it. The Example Pace GPS Watch manages both, and it does so with a display that makes pre-dawn glances effortless.

## Fit & Feel

At 43 grams the case sits low and light on the wrist, and the soft silicone strap has enough holes to fit both slim and broad wrists. We wore it around the clock for three weeks, including overnight sleep tracking, without hot spots or irritation. Five physical buttons handle everything during a run, so there is no fighting a touchscreen with sweaty or gloved fingers.

## On the Run

Start-up is quick: satellite lock typically arrived within ten seconds in dual-band mode. The data screens are fully customisable, and the large AMOLED digits are readable at a glance even under streetlights and in the flat blue light before sunrise.

The optical heart-rate sensor tracked steady efforts closely against a chest strap and only lagged on sharp interval surges, which is typical for wrist-based sensors. Structured workouts are easy to build in the app and sync reliably, with clear vibration alerts for each step.

The training metrics are what keep us coming back. A morning readiness score blends sleep, heart-rate variability and recent load into one simple number, and it was right more often than not: on the days it told us to take it easy, our legs agreed by the second mile. Post-run summaries highlight training effect and recovery time without burying you in charts, which makes it easier to stick to a plan rather than chase every number.

## Battery & GPS

Dual-band GPS is the headline here. On a route that weaves between tall buildings and under heavy tree cover, the Pace drew tracks that hugged the road, with lap distances within a few metres of a measured track. Pace readings were stable rather than jumpy, which makes running by feel and by number much easier.

Battery life is strong. With the display in gesture-wake mode we averaged 12 to 14 days of mixed use including five hours of weekly GPS training. Switch on always-on display and that drops to around six days, so most runners will want to leave it off.

## Who It's For

This watch suits runners who care about accurate pace and distance, want meaningful recovery guidance, and would rather not pay flagship prices. It is equally at home on roads and moderate trails. If you need onboard music or full mapping for ultra-distance navigation, step up to a higher tier; for everyone else, the Example Pace GPS Watch covers the essentials brilliantly.
