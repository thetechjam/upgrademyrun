## How We Chose

Every product in this guide earned its place on the road, not on a spec sheet. Each pick was used across several weeks of normal training by multiple testers, covering easy runs, long runs, wet mornings and the pitch-dark starts that define winter mileage. We logged comfort, reliability and how often we reached for each item without being asked to.

We weighted the everyday experience most heavily. A shoe that feels great for a single tempo session but punishes you on recovery days does not belong here. Neither does a watch with impressive features buried behind confusing menus. The goal was a kit that works together and stays out of your way: a shoe for most of your miles, a watch that tells you the truth, and headphones that keep you safe.

Price mattered too. Nothing here is the most expensive option in its category, and every pick delivers performance that holds up against gear costing considerably more.

## What to Look For

**Shoes:** Prioritise comfort and stability over weight for daily training. A moderate drop, durable rubber and a secure midfoot fit will serve you better than the latest racing foam on most days.

**Watches:** Look for dual-band or multi-band GPS if you run among trees or tall buildings, and check real-world battery life with your preferred display settings rather than the headline figure.

**Headphones:** If you run on roads, open-ear or bone-conduction designs keep you aware of traffic. Check the water rating, and make sure the controls are physical buttons you can find without looking.

**Visibility and weather:** Whatever you choose, reflective details and some water resistance go a long way when most of your running happens before sunrise.
